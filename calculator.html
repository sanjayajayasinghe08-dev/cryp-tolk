How It Works

3 Inputs → Instant Leverage

1
Enter Your Risk Amount ($)

This is the maximum dollar amount you are willing to lose if your stop-loss is hit. A common rule is to risk no more than 1–2% of your total account balance per trade. For a $5,000 account at 1% risk, this would be $50.

2
Enter Your Margin / Position Size ($)

This is the collateral you are depositing into the position — the actual dollar value of margin used. This is NOT your total position value (which would be margin × leverage). Enter the cash margin before leverage is applied.

3
Enter Your Stop-Loss Distance (%)

This is how far your stop-loss is from your entry price, expressed as a percentage. For example, if you enter a BTC long at $100,000 and place a stop at $97,500, your stop distance is 2.5%. The tighter the stop, the higher the leverage required.

The Formula Explained
# Exact Leverage
Leverage = Risk $ ÷ (Margin $ × Stop%)
# Example
50 ÷ (200 × 0.025) = 10x

The Exact Leverage is the precise mathematical leverage required for your risk parameters. The Safe Setup rounds this down to the nearest whole number — always erring on the side of lower risk. Never round up: using higher leverage than calculated increases your actual risk beyond your intended limit.

Pro Risk Management Tips

1–2% Rule: Never risk more than 1–2% of your total trading capital on a single trade. On a $5,000 account, that's $50–$100 maximum per position.

Stop Behind Structure: Always place your stop-loss behind a key support level, order block, or liquidity zone — not at an arbitrary percentage. Then use this calculator to find the correct leverage for that stop distance.

Minimum 2R Target: Only enter a trade if your take-profit is at least 2× your risk distance (2:1 reward-to-risk). This means even a 40% win rate produces profits over time.

High Leverage Warning: If the calculator returns more than 20x leverage, reconsider the trade. Either your stop is too tight, your risk amount is too high relative to margin, or you need a larger capital base. High leverage multiplies losses just as fast as gains.

Understanding Leverage in Crypto Trading

Leverage in cryptocurrency trading is a tool that allows traders to control a position larger than their actual capital. When you use 10× leverage on a $200 margin deposit, you are controlling a $2,000 position. This amplifies both gains and losses — a 1% price move in your favor generates a 10% return on your margin, but a 1% adverse move results in a 10% loss.

The key insight that most retail traders miss is that leverage should always be determined by your stop-loss placement, not your profit target. The correct approach is to first identify where your stop-loss should logically be placed based on market structure (below a key support level, order block, or liquidity zone), then use this calculator to determine what leverage produces your desired dollar risk at that stop distance.

This is the inverse of how most beginners think about leverage. They choose a leverage level first (e.g., "I'll use 20x") and then wonder why their stop gets hit so quickly. The professional approach defines risk first, stop placement second, and calculates leverage as an output — never an input.

Example: You have a $500 trading account and want to risk 2% ($10) on a BTC long. You identify a key support level that requires a stop-loss 1.5% below your entry. Plugging these values in: Leverage = $10 ÷ ($100 margin × 0.015) = 6.67x. The safe setup would be 6x leverage. This gives you a precisely risk-controlled position regardless of the volatility of the asset.

Why does the calculator round DOWN for the safe leverage?

Rounding down always keeps your actual risk at or below your intended risk amount. If the exact leverage is 8.7x and you round UP to 9x, you would be risking slightly more than your intended dollar amount if the stop is hit. Rounding down to 8x ensures your loss is always within your risk tolerance — a small concession in potential gain for a guaranteed protection of capital.

What's a good stop-loss percentage for BTC and ETH?

There is no universal "correct" stop-loss percentage — it depends entirely on the timeframe you're trading and the nearest structural level below (for longs) or above (for shorts) your entry. On the daily chart, a 3–8% stop is common for BTC. On the 4-hour, 1.5–4% is typical. On the 1-hour, 0.5–2%. The stop should always be placed at a technical level that invalidates your trade thesis, not at an arbitrary percentage.

Can I use this calculator for both spot and futures trading?

This calculator is designed primarily for leveraged futures/perpetual contract trading where margin and leverage are applied. For spot trading, leverage is always 1x — you can't lose more than your position value. However, the risk calculation concept still applies: you can use the formula to determine your ideal position size in spot trading by setting leverage to 1x and calculating what dollar amount of spot to buy given your risk budget and stop placement.
